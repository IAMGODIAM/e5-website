Programs of E5 Enclave Inc. · The Block Program

From the block,
to the boardroom.

Block to the Boardroom is E5 Enclave’s program for the neighborhoods that raised us: rehabilitating Black neighborhood blocks with emerging technology, building learn-to-earn STEM and Web3 pipelines, and moving the land beneath our feet back into community hands. The block is the foundation. The boardroom is where its builders take their seats.

The plan

Three strands, one block

Block to the Boardroom gathers the “block” lineage of E5’s founding documents into a single program — three strands of work that turn a neighborhood block from a place people leave into a place people own.

01 Block rehabilitation

Building Better Blocks

Bring emerging technology to the neighborhood block: connected, efficient, sustainable urban environments that support Black-owned businesses — business incubators, affordable housing, STEM workforce development, and sensor networks woven through the streetscape.

The concept originates in Building Better Blocks (B3), a project document dated January 2023 — a proposal, not an operating program.

02 Learn-to-earn pipeline

Black Block Builders

Advanced STEM education and Web3 fluency — blockchain, cryptography, no-code tools, artificial intelligence — delivered as learn-to-earn pathways. Participants earn while they learn the skills of the digital economy, then deploy them in peer-to-peer marketplaces and community exchanges.

The concept originates in Black Block Builders, an SBIR-style small-business research grant bid developed through Web 333 LLC — a proposal, not an operating program.

03 Land-buying engine

Gentrification reversal

Revenue and resources directed at the oldest form of wealth: land. The program’s economic logic is to purchase land and invest it in the community — increasing Black ownership of homes, businesses, and land, and reversing the displacement that “revitalization” usually brings.

The mechanism is named in the founding documents: profits from community enterprise converted into land and sustainable wealth-building, held by the community.

The reversal engine

When a block gets better, its people usually get pushed out.

This program inverts that sequence. Rehabilitation raises the block’s value; community ownership captures it. The discipline runs from the small to the large: fix the broken window before the block follows — maintenance as anti-displacement policy, every vacant lot a future garden or lab instead of a developer’s option.

As value returns to the block, the land-buying engine converts it into ownership that cannot be flipped away: homes, businesses, and land held by the people who live on the block.

Geography

Pine Bluff, the replication narrative

Pine Bluff, Arkansas is the named pilot site in the source documents. The B3 document names it as a potential site for the first rehabilitated block; Project Reconstruction and Repair is a full proposal for it — an 8,000-square-foot organic garden, twelve double-decker container housing units, advanced STEM education, and a community mesh network.

None of it is operating. There is no E5 activity in Pine Bluff today. The proposal is preserved here as the replication narrative — the blueprint the model could travel in, once it is proven in Miami.

Lineage

The seed

The vision began in a recorded conversation in March 2022: buy the vacant lots instead of watching them decay, turn empty buildings into homes, gardens feeding food banks, learn-to-earn coding for neighborhood youth. Every document that followed descends from those ideas.

Only the ideas appear on this page. Specific personal claims from that conversation are not presented as fact.

Honest accounting

What “proposed” means

None of the figures above describe money raised, a program operating, or outcomes achieved. There is no lab, garden, or housing unit operating under this program today.

Any page, post, or person describing Block to the Boardroom as an operating program is mistaken — including earlier versions of this page, which have been corrected.

The path in is the Coalition.

Block to the Boardroom is built by the people it serves. If you want to train, to teach, to open a boardroom door — or to bring a block with you — the way in is the Coalition. Tell them you’re coming for Block to the Boardroom.
