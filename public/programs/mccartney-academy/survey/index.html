Where this stands

The honest timeline.

The school does not exist yet — no building, no operator, no programs. What exists is the work: a charter application to the school district, with a target opening after that.

The record

Why the name McCartney.

McCartney Academy is named for a man whose service is on the public record — honored on the floor of Congress, proclaimed by a city, and preserved in his own words. We state here only what the record establishes.

Congressional Record

“Tribute to Ralph McCartney”

Hon. Carrie P. Meek · U.S. House of Representatives
February 1, 1994 · Vol. 140, pp. 602–603

“That I was a McCartney.”

The tribute records his birth on March 11, 1934, the last of eight children of Leon and Lillian McCartney, in the Overtown community — and three of the works he carried out there: the pedestrian overpass at Edison Park Elementary across I-95, the Department of Defense Race Relations Institute, and the rebuilding of Booker T. Washington.

  • 1994Honored on the floor of the U.S. House by Rep. Carrie P. Meek of Florida.
  • RolesUrban League of Greater Miami; Dade County Community Relations Board; Economic Opportunity Program, Inc.; director of the Neighbor Center; Rev. Leon Sullivan’s OIC of Dade County.

Oral history

“Tell the Story”

UF Samuel Proctor Oral History Program
Recorded August 14, 1997 · Interviewer: Devon Williams

Ralph McCartney sat for a full oral-history interview — his life, in his own words, recorded for the archive. The transcript runs fifty-eight pages.

City proclamation

“A Community of Brotherhood and Sisterhood”

City of Opa-locka · May 25, 1994
Mayor Robert B. Ingram

Three months after the congressional tribute, the City of Opa-locka proclaimed its own honor — a community of brotherhood and sisterhood, in his name.
