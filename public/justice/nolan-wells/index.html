Nolan Wells was eighteen years old. He was found in the water off Horn Island on the morning of July 6, 2026. Two forensic pathologists examined him. One worked for the State of Mississippi. One was retained independently, by counsel for his family.

The Mississippi State Medical Examiner's Office ruled cause and manner of death undetermined. Dr. Roger A. Mitchell, the independently retained forensic pathologist, reached the same conclusion — undetermined, pending further investigation — and transmitted his findings to the family's counsel on July 19, 2026.

Both examinations found bruising to the back of his head. The State's report states the injury was not fatal. Neither report explains it. The grand jury reported that it found no other evidence accounting for the bruising, and that it could not say with certainty what caused it.

On September 21, 2026, the office of District Attorney Angel Myers McIlrath announced that a grand jury of twenty-three had unanimously returned a no true bill. It had issued 132 subpoenas. It had heard 43 witnesses. Seven people were invited to testify. No charges were returned against anyone.

The report released to the public that night is redacted. The testimony of the 43 witnesses is sealed. The full autopsy file has not been released.

This page will not fill that gap with a theory. The demand is not for a conclusion. It is for the file — unredacted, complete, and in front of experts the State did not choose.

§03 The SilenceOpen questions · unanswered

A file can be closed without being answered.

These are not allegations. Every one of them is a question the released record leaves standing. We put them in public because a question in public is harder to seal than a question in a drawer.

Updated as the record moves. If the office releases more, this section changes.

Q.01

What caused the injury to the back of his head?

Both pathologists found it. The State's report says it was not fatal. The grand jury reported it found no other evidence to explain the bruising and could not say with certainty what caused it. That is the whole of the public answer.

Q.02

Why is the publicly released report redacted?

The inquiry is over. No one was charged. No prosecution stands to be prejudiced. Name the interest that redaction protects now, and name who it protects it from.

Q.03

Why is the testimony of forty-three witnesses sealed?

Forty-three people told the State what they knew, under oath. The public that convened the grand jury, paid for the investigation, and is asked to accept its result is not permitted to read a word of it.

Q.04

Why has the full autopsy file not been released?

Two pathologists reached the same conclusion from it: undetermined. One of them called it pending further investigation. Further investigation requires the file. Release it — to the family's experts, and to the public.

Q.05

What is an eighteen-year-old's death worth, as a matter of procedure?

This is the question underneath the other four. A system that can spend 132 subpoenas and forty-three witnesses and still hand the public a redacted page has not run out of capacity. It has run out of will.

§04 The DemandE5 Enclave · Black Lives Matter Grassroots

Four things, in plain terms, on the record.

In re: Nolan Wells/Jackson County, Mississippi/Lawful · Non-partisan · Sustained
Demand I

Release the unredacted report.

The grand jury has concluded. No one was charged. No prosecution can be prejudiced by disclosure. Publish the report as written, without redaction, and let the public read what its own grand jury was told.

Demand II

Release the full autopsy file.

Not the summary. The complete file — photographs, slides, measurements, toxicology, the full report of examination. One of the two pathologists called his own conclusion pending further investigation. That investigation cannot proceed on a summary.

Demand III

Independent forensic review.

Two pathologists declined to state a cause and a manner of death, and both found an injury neither explained. That is the definition of a case that needs another set of eyes — chosen by someone other than the State, with access to everything.

Demand IV · Do this today

Contact the Jackson County District Attorney.

The office that released the redacted report is the office that can release the rest of it. Be brief, be lawful, be specific: the unredacted report, the full autopsy file, independent forensic review.
