## Finding 1 — Reciprocation moves money (Cialdini #1)
- Disabled American Veterans: adding unsolicited personalized address labels nearly **doubled** donation success, 18% → 35%.
- Reciprocal concessions (door-in-the-face): compliance **tripled**, 17% → 50%.
- **Page application:** the $5 starter chip is our low-friction entry (concession architecture); the stewardship promise ("we report back") is reciprocity in reverse — we give first (receipt, annual impact letter, Form 990 access), which obligates return giving.

## Finding 2 — Micro-commitments direct future action (Cialdini #2)
- Residents who signed a petition two weeks earlier **nearly doubled** contributions; restaurant "will you please call if you change plans?" + pause cut no-shows 30% → 10%.
- Influence Ch. 1: giving a **reason** ("because") measurably raises compliance.
- **Page application:** monthly stays preselected (a standing commitment frame) with an explicit because-reason ("Monthly is preselected because steady funding builds steady infrastructure"); one-time givers get an honest upgrade nudge, never a trap.

## Finding 3 — Social proof is double-edged; trends beat static norms (Cialdini #1, Mortensen et al. 2019)
- A fundraiser showing homeowners a list of neighbors who donated **significantly increased** contributions — the longer the list, the greater the effect. But campaigns that depict an undesirable behavior as frequent **backfire** ("look at all the people doing this undesirable thing" reads as permission).
- Mortensen et al.: static minority norms backfire by establishing a norm of *non*-engagement; **trending** norms (a behavior *increasing* in popularity) spur conformity even when only a minority does it, because people predict the trend will continue. Small effects matter at scale (their Experiment 2: 8–10% donation lift).
- **Page application:** NO fabricated donor counts, totals raised, or "most donors" claims. The only trend language on the page is sector-level and cited: monthly giving revenue +5% while one-time stayed flat (M+R Benchmarks 2025); record $592.5B US giving (Giving USA 2025); DAF grants +19% (Annual DAF Report 2025).

## Finding 4 — Trust infrastructure is the conversion engine (Cialdini #1, accounting guide, Donor Bill of Rights)
- Authority must be **real** (phony authority steers donors wrong).
- Nonprofit accounting's first principle: **accountability over profitability**; restricted gifts must be honored and reported; Form 990 is the public's window into revenue, salaries, and allocation; audits are trust signals.
- The Donor Bill of Rights (10 rights): mission/use/capacity disclosure; board identity + prudent stewardship; access to recent financial statements; gifts used for stated purposes; acknowledgement; confidentiality; professional relationships; solicitor identity; mailing-list deletion; prompt truthful answers to questions.
- **Page application:** render the actual Donor Bill of Rights on the page (new `#rights` section, attributed); keep EIN 99-3822441, 501(c)(3), Form 990 inspection, UEI/CAGE/SAM.gov front and center; "every gift is used for the purpose you gave it" as the restricted-gift promise.

## Finding 5 — Ask architecture follows the pipeline (DonorSearch #6)
- Pipeline: identification → qualification → cultivation → **stewardship**; segment asks by new / repeat / upgradeable / high-lifetime-value; predictive models match the ask to capacity.
- **Page application:** entry ($5 chip) → standard tiers → leadership tiers ($250/$500 monthly, Stripe slots staged) → major vehicles (DAF, stock, planned, matching, tribute, in-kind) → stewardship loop (reporting back converts one-time into lifetime value).

## Finding 6 — Voice: people-powered, action-menu clarity (Revolution Love #7)
- Authoritative, compassionate, progressive; assertive yet inclusive; "grassroots, people-powered movement not funded by Super-PACs and corporations"; merchandise/actions as funding; every ask paired with a concrete action.
- **Page application:** honest people-powered framing (E5 is grassroots — true); the six non-card offerings each carry one clear next step (a pre-addressed email to contact@e5enclave.com), i.e. an action menu, not a brochure.

## What this brief forbids
- Fabricated donor counts, amounts raised, "trending among our donors" claims.
- Static minority-norm framing ("most people don't give, but you can").
- Manufactured scarcity or urgency.
- Tax advice beyond general, hedged information ("ask your advisor").
- Any new Stripe tier presented as live before its payment link exists (slots render only when URLs are present — no dead buttons).
