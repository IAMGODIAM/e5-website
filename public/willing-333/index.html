The Willing 333

Technology in Service of Neighborhoods

E5 Enclave is inviting 333 companies to help build a vendor-agnostic Civic Robotics Commons for Miami-Dade: a human-commanded fleet of robots, drones, land-care machines, digital tools, and trained people working together to make neighborhoods cleaner, greener, safer, and more capable.

The Invitation

One useful part of a neighborhood-service commons.

Donate, lend, train, credit, integrate, insure, or sponsor one useful part of a human-commanded neighborhood-service commons. Start with one safe, measurable Miami work cell — and publish what we learn.

The Commons is vendor-agnostic by design. No single supplier owns the fleet, the data, or the mission. Every contribution is bounded by manufacturer limits, insurance, training, weather, site, and permit requirements — and every mission is planned by people, governed by a written work order, and recorded in a privacy-preserving mission log.

The name is aspirational. Being named among the 333 means a company is being invited. It does not imply endorsement, commitment, participation, or partnership by any company. No company is described as participating until a verified commitment exists.

The Flagship

Clean + Green: the first twelve months.

The first flagship focuses on bounded, permitted work: litter and debris collection; vegetation and planting support; condition mapping; materials movement; and minor repair demonstrations where licensing, property consent, and safety controls allow.

Hazardous, regulated, structural, electrical, plumbing, pesticide, aerial, or public-right-of-way work remains with properly licensed and authorized parties — always.

Mission control

Human-commanded, always

Every mission is planned by people and governed by a written work order. Automated systems remain human-commanded; regulated work remains with qualified parties.

Civic Action Desk

Field learning becomes civic action

Field observations flow to the Civic Action Desk, where staff and software agents identify city-service requests, grant opportunities, maintenance needs, and lawful land pathways.

Commons Lab + Academy

Learning that replicates

Field learning converts into resident training, replicable procedures, and interoperable technology — so the commons grows people, not just machines.

Non-Negotiable Terms

The safeguards are the product.

These operating terms are not negotiable. They are what make the commons worthy of a neighborhood's trust.

  • E5 retains mission command, work-order approval, community consent, and the right to stop work.
  • No weapons, policing, biometric identification, facial recognition, covert surveillance, or resident scoring.
  • No hazardous-material handling by unlicensed people or machines; suspicious material is isolated and referred.
  • Devices must support least-privilege access, audit logs, data export, deletion, and defined retention.
  • Public communications distinguish donated, loaned, discounted, and sponsored contributions.
  • Partners receive agreed recognition and outcome reporting — never editorial control over community findings.
  • E5 avoids exclusivity that would undermine interoperability, resilience, or public trust.

The Path

Ninety days from invitation to demonstration.

Days 1–15

Authorize and package

Approve the mission boundaries, prohibited uses, data rules, and partner agreements. Build the brief, the ledger, and this page.

Days 16–30

Validate the field cell

Select one permissioned pilot site, complete mapping, listening, risk assessment, permits, and insurance. Rehearse people-only before introducing machines.

Days 31–60

Approach the Founding 33

Tailored outreach in three waves of eleven, tracked from introduction through decision — seeking a balanced minimum viable coalition.

Days 61–90

Demonstrate and publish

One supervised micro-pilot with explicit stop criteria, then a plain-language outcome note: what was served, removed, planted, trained — and learned.
